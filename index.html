    Research
    Which IoT sites should a federated IDS trust?
    My M.Tech research: intrusion detection trained across IoT and medical-IoT sites that can't share traffic, where some sites see rare attacks and some hold noisy or poisoned data.
    

      
    
        
    
          IEEE paper draft · 2026
          
    From Heuristic to Calibrated Client Weighting: ARCS-FL, ARCS-FL v2 and PRISM-FL for Federated Intrusion Detection on Non-IID and Unreliable IoT Data
    
          
    The server's aggregation weights decide how much each site counts. Standard rules either ignore heterogeneity or treat all of it as low quality, so a hospital that is the only one seeing a rare attack gets drowned out. The paper separates benign label skew, which should be kept or amplified, from harmful corruption, which should be attenuated or bounded, through three successively stronger weighting rules.
    
        
    
        
      
          
    1. 1ARCS-FLData size × reliability × diversity × consistency. Intuitive and cheap, but every factor mistakes legitimate label skew for low quality.
      2. 
          
    2. 2ARCS-FL v2Repairs each factor with something the server can verify: prototype-based reliability, shift-aware diversity, a median/MAD outlier test and norm-bounded updates.
      3. 
          
    3. 3PRISM-FLReplaces the heuristic with a strongly convex calibration program with proven guarantees. It reduces exactly to FedAvg when nothing is wrong.
      4. 
        
    
        
    3 datasets: IoTID20, CICIoMT2024, Edge-IIoTset16 aggregation rules4 heterogeneity levels4 corruption families≈4,800 federated runs

          Also from the paper
          
    A data-leakage trap in a common IoTID20 pipeline
    
          
    A widely used IoTID20 preprocessing pipeline leaves 52.0% of test rows with an exact twin in the training set, which inflates reported accuracy. The benchmark in this paper is leakage-audited, tunes every method on validation data only, and tests significance with Friedman/Nemenyi and Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests.
    
          
    
            
    Benign label shiftSites see different attack mixes, so keep or amplify their updates
    
            
    Label corruptionNoisy or flipped labels, so attenuate those sites
    
            
    Model poisoningGaussian or sign-flipped updates, so bound their influence
    
            
    Class prototypesShared-model prototypes tell skew and corruption apart
    
          
    
          
    The prototype-based noise detector separates noisy sites with AUROC 0.98. A comparable local-prototype method (GSWA) scores 0.20.
